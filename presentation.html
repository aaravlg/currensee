01 · The Problem We're Solving

Can You Tell Notes Apart With Your Eyes Closed?

Millions of visually impaired people in India handle currency every day — buying groceries, paying for a rickshaw, receiving change — often having to trust someone else to tell them which note is which.

02 · Collecting the Data

Teaching a Machine to "See" Money

Before a machine can recognize anything, it has to be shown many examples — just like how we learn to recognize a friend's face after seeing them a few times.

🔬 Fair testing: we kept the number of images equal across all classes — an unequal dataset would bias the model toward whichever note it saw most, just like an unfair experiment with uneven trials.
03 · The Science of Recognition

How a Machine Learns to Classify Images

A computer doesn't "see" a note the way we do — it sees a grid of numbers (pixel values for color and brightness). The model learns which number patterns tend to appear together for each note.

Just like our eyes & brain

  • Our eyes detect light and color (retina)
  • Our brain recognizes patterns (visual cortex)
  • The model does the same, just with maths instead of neurons

Why we didn't start from zero

  • We reused a model already trained on millions of general images
  • Only fine-tuned it for currency notes — much faster
  • This is called Transfer Learning
🧪 Core concept: Pattern recognition + statistics — the model finds the color, shape, and texture patterns that reliably separate one note from another.
04 · What Happens When You Scan

From Camera Frame to Spoken Word — Live

All of this happens inside the phone's browser itself — no internet needed once the page is open, and nothing is sent anywhere.

1️⃣ Camera captures a live frame of the note
2️⃣ The trained model checks the pattern against all 7 classes
3️⃣ It picks the class it's most confident about
4️⃣ If confidence is too low → says "please try again" (avoids wrong guesses)
5️⃣ Otherwise → speaks the result aloud instantly
05 · Impact & Real-World Use

Why This Matters Beyond the Exhibition

  • No new hardware needed — works on any phone browser people already own
  • Works offline after the page loads once — reliable even without good internet
  • Pairs with tools already used — like phone screen readers, for a smoother experience
  • Extendable — could later support coins, other currencies, or counterfeit-feature checks
🌍 Applied science in action: combining optics (camera), computer science (pattern recognition), and accessibility engineering to solve a genuine everyday problem.
